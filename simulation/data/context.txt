Think of it as a three-part “lock‑in” process layered on top of your magnetic shaping:

Mix in a small reactive/binder phase.
The regolith isn’t pure dust; a fraction of grains (or coatings) are alkali/glass‑rich so that, when warmed, they can partially dissolve and re‑solidify at contacts, like a low‑temperature ceramic glue.

Hold the shaped object gently with fields while you “bake” it.
After your code has formed the hollow/freeform shape, you keep a weaker magnetic configuration on (plus small vibrations) and heat the region to only ~150–300 °C, not 1000+ °C. The heat plus mild contact stress lets those reactive phases form solid necks between grains at their contact points.

Grow a bonded network between grains.
Over tens of minutes, more and more contacts convert from “just touching” to “bonded” so the particle assembly becomes a self‑supporting solid shell that keeps its shape even when you turn fields off. In simulation, you model this as each contact getting a bond-strength variable that increases with temperature, time in contact, and mild pressure.

So: you first shape with magnetics, then you chemically lock the structure at low temperature using a sparse binder that activates only where grains touch, without needing a mold or high global pressure.
